Reverse vaccinology makes long lists. Labs need short ones.

Screening a genome for vaccine antigens is fast. Finding out which hits survive an assay is slow, and most published tools never say how often they are right.

1.2%experimental hit rate in the canonical meningococcus B study
13 to 15 yrfrom that genome paper to an approved vaccine

What it did on a real run

One recorded run on a stored subset of Neisseria meningitidis. Every figure is read from the saved output of that run.

  • 40 of 2,003proteins screened (stored fixture subset of strain MC58)
  • 27 of 30survive the four safety gates, with real tools
  • 98.5%projected world class I coverage of the epitope set
  • 36 of 36template-built robot programs pass the Opentrons simulator
  • blockedS12 biosecurity gate: not cleared for synthesis

Candidates, not a vaccine. Nothing here is a measured assay result. The antigen ranking is a hypothesis, and on the one retrieval test we ran, the three antigens of the licensed Bexsero vaccine ranked 7th, 9th and 14th (recall@3 of 0 of 3, and circular). The page says so on purpose.
